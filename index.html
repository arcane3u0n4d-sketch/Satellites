What Is a Satellite?

A satellite is an object that orbits a larger object in space. Natural satellites include the Moon. Artificial satellites are human-made machines placed into orbit around Earth or other worlds.

Uses of Satellites

  • Weather forecasting and cyclone monitoring.
  • GPS navigation and location services.
  • Television, telephone and internet communications.
  • Observing forests, oceans and Earth's climate.
  • Scientific research and space exploration.
  • Disaster management and emergency communication.

Types of Artificial Satellites

Communication satellites: Transmit television, telephone and internet signals.

Weather satellites: Observe clouds, storms and atmospheric conditions.

Navigation satellites: Help receivers calculate positions and routes.

Earth observation satellites: Collect images and measurements of our planet.

Scientific satellites: Study space, stars and planets.

India's Satellite Programme

ISRO, the Indian Space Research Organisation, develops and operates satellites for communication, navigation, weather observation and scientific research.

Examples include the INSAT, GSAT, NavIC and Cartosat satellite systems.

How Do Satellites Stay in Orbit?

Gravity pulls a satellite toward the object it orbits. Its forward motion makes it continually fall around that object instead of immediately falling to the ground.
